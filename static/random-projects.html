Knitting Machine Restoration

I bought a vintage Singer Memo-Matic 327 in 2023, which is certainly way older than I was. This machine is purely mechanical and can be used to produce knitted fabric manually. I ended up spending far more time deep-cleaning it, scouring eBay for replacement parts, and taking the machine apart and putting it back together than actually using it. This made me realize, perhaps for the first time, that I probably enjoy the process of tinkering and figuring out how things work just as much as, if not more than, the end result.

I also built the table that holds the knitting machine myself, combining a sturdy BORA workhorse with an IKEA tabletop. This was necessary because you have to swing the knitting machine carriage from left to right to knit, so you need a stable surface that won't wobble like a typical table might.

Unfortunately, I didn't document the step-by-step process, but here's a summary of what I did:

  • Replaced all the needles in the knitting machine. I tried to use rust remover on some of the needles, but overdid it, and they became brittle.
  • Replaced the sponge bar.
  • Disassembled the carriage and gave its components a good bath in Marvel Mystery Oil.
  • Re-oiled the moving parts with Hoppe's gun oil. This type of oil has a low viscosity, which helps prevent gumming and residue buildup over time.
  • Learned that when working with the knitting machine screws, it's important to use JIS (Japanese Industrial Standard) screwdrivers rather than Phillips-head screwdrivers. They may look almost identical, but using the wrong one can damage the screw heads.

There are surprisingly few resources available online about restoring a knitting machine. I ended up cold-DMing a lovely older lady who was kind enough to help me troubleshoot some of the issues I ran into, watching a handful of random YouTube videos, and, of course, spending quite some time reading through the original black-and-white instruction manual for this machine.

DIY Tempeh

Successfully made tempeh from scratch using soybeans and tempeh starter! The most time-consuming part was dehulling the soybeans.

I went down an internet rabbit hole and found an Indonesian living in the US who built a custom prototype machine to split soybeans (YouTube video). I even reached out to him, but couldn't justify spending a couple hundred dollars on one. If you're interested, you can reach out to him too! Another option is using a grain mill, although they aren't ideal for soybeans and may split them into more pieces.

I also experimented with making tempeh using red beans!

Cross-Country Amtrak Trip

In 2024, a friend and I set out on a three-week cross-country Amtrak adventure, starting from Baltimore!

Here's the route we took:

Baltimore → Chicago → Whitefish (for Glacier National Park) → Seattle (for Olympic National Park) → San Francisco (for Yosemite National Park) → Utah (for Zion and Bryce Canyon National Parks) → Chicago → Baltimore.

It's certainly doable to sleep in Amtrak's coach seats. I brought a pillow, an eye mask, and earplugs, which helped me get a pretty good night's sleep overall.

What about hygiene? Some train rides are really long. For example, the journey from Chicago to Whitefish takes around 32 hours! For those extra-long rides, I used rinse-free bath sponges to freshen up without needing a shower. You just need to add a little water before using them.

What about food? We relied quite a bit on the cafe cars, which actually served pretty decent food, including burgers, cup noodles, and sandwiches. We also stocked up on snacks from grocery stores along the way, such as nuts and bananas.

How about entertainment? I saw some people bringing cards and other games to pass the time. Personally, I was very happy sitting by the window in the observation car, enjoying the scenery while reading or writing.

A few tips: Look into the Amtrak USA Rail Pass, which allows you to take multiple train segments over a 30-day period. Amtrak sometimes offers discounts during the winter, so it's worth keeping an eye out for promotions!

Meal Prep Sundays

I like to prepare my meals in advance, especially since I tend to have busy weeks. It's nice not having to worry about cooking every day and simply being able to microwave a frozen meal when I'm hungry.

Another reason I meal prep is that I enjoy lifting weights. To support muscle recovery, I try to keep track of how much protein I consume each day. Cooking in batches also makes it much easier to calculate the macros for each meal.

I like using MyFoodData's Daily Food Log to calculate and keep track of my macros.

Honestly, these days, my meals have become increasingly simple, so there's not quite as much variety as you might see in the picture anymore. I eat a lot of vegetables, sweet potatoes, chicken breast, white fish, and egg whites. I also regularly have cottage cheese, Greek yogurt, walnuts, avocados, oats, chia seeds, psyllium husk, and whey protein powder.

Of course, I eat out too and have cheat days, perhaps more often than I would like to admit, haha.
