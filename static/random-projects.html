Knitting Machine Restoration

I bought a vintage Singer Memo-Matic 327, which is certainly way older than I am. I ended up spending far more time deep-cleaning it, scouring eBay for replacement parts, and taking the machine apart and putting it back together than actually using it. This made me realize, perhaps for the first time, that I probably enjoy the process of tinkering, troubleshooting, and figuring out how things work just as much as, if not more than, the end result.

I also built the table that holds the knitting machine myself, combining a sturdy BORA workhorse with an IKEA tabletop. Unfortunately, I didn't document the step-by-step process, but here's a summary of what I did:

  • Replaced all the needles in the knitting machine. I tried to use rust remover on some of the needles, but overdid them, and they became brittle.
  • Replaced the sponge bar.
  • Disassembled the carriage and gave its components a good bath in Marvel Mystery Oil.
  • Re-oiled the moving parts with Hoppe's gun oil.
  • Learned that when working with the screws, it's important to use JIS (Japanese Industrial Standard) screwdrivers rather than Phillips-head screwdrivers. They may look almost identical, but using the wrong one can damage the screw heads.

There are surprisingly few resources available online about restoring this particular machine. I ended up cold-DMing a lovely older lady who was kind enough to help me troubleshoot some of the issues I ran into, watching a handful of random YouTube videos, and, of course, spending quite some time reading through the original black-and-white instruction manual that came with the machine.
