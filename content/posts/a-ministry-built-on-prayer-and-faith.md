As some of you may know, I've been rereading *The Autobiography of George Müller*. For whatever reason, I had always thought that his main reason for establishing the orphan houses was to care for poor children and teach them about God. However, I recently realized that this was not his primary purpose. In his own words:

> I certainly desire to be used by God to help the poor children and train them in the ways of God. But the primary object of the work is that God would be magnified because the orphans under my care will be provided with all they need **through prayer and faith**. Everyone will see that God is faithful and hears prayer.

I found this to be such a profound perspective. Rather than seeing his ministry merely as a way to help the children, he was even more concerned with making God's faithfulness and willingness to answer prayer known through the orphan houses. And this makes sense. It would bring greater glory to God if the work could be attributed entirely to His provision rather than to human ability or resources. Ultimately, by pursuing that very objective, the needs of the children in the orphan houses established by George Müller were also met.

May I grow in my trust in God's promise that if we seek His Kingdom and His righteousness, all these things will be added to us (Matthew 6:33). May I grow in discernment about what truly matters to God and what brings Him the most glory, rather than being driven by my own ambitions. May He give me a simple, childlike faith that allows me to take His promises at face value.
