My recent Bible readings have been taking me through the book of Acts, and I find that with every rereading, my admiration for Barnabas only grows. In chapter 4, where he is first introduced, we are told that he sold a field that belonged to him and brought the money to the apostles' feet.

My first impression? How generous of him. And as I continued reading, following the conversion of Saul in chapter 9, Barnabas was the one who took Saul and brought him to the apostles, vouching for him when the other disciples in Jerusalem were afraid of Saul and doubted whether he was truly a disciple.

And then, in chapter 11, Barnabas was sent to Antioch. Verse 23 tells us, "When he came and saw the grace of God, he was glad, and he exhorted them all to remain faithful to the Lord with steadfast purpose." I find this to be such a beautiful quality in Barnabas: his ability to genuinely rejoice in what God was doing in other people's lives and to delight in seeing others grow in the Lord, without seeking attention for himself. The very next verse describes him as "a good man, full of the Holy Spirit and faith." This also brings to mind 1 Timothy 3:7, which highlights the importance of having a good reputation as one of the qualifications for overseers.

And remember Saul (later Paul), the man he vouched for in chapter 9? Barnabas went out of his way to look for him in Tarsus, and when he found Saul, he brought him to Antioch. Together, they taught many people for an entire year. And so, Barnabas believed in Saul not only with words, but also through his actions. He actively took him under his wing, giving him room to grow and serve alongside him.

And lastly, in chapters 12 and 13, we see that both Paul and Barnabas took John, also called Mark, with them on their missionary journey. However, John Mark eventually left them at Perga in Pamphylia and returned to Jerusalem. And so, in chapter 15, when Paul and Barnabas were planning another journey through the cities where they had previously preached, Paul disagreed with Barnabas when he suggested taking Mark along again. After all, Mark had a history of leaving them during their previous journey. The disagreement actually became so sharp that they had to go their separate ways, with Barnabas taking Mark to Cyprus and Paul taking Silas through Syria and Cilicia.

I like to think that both Paul and Barnabas discerned correctly what they ought to do. Perhaps Paul discerned that he ought to focus on the missionary work ahead with someone he could rely on. On the other hand, perhaps Barnabas felt called to mentor Mark, someone who needed another opportunity to grow, just as Saul once needed another person to believe in him. And in the end, both paths served a purpose. We later see Paul himself describing Mark as someone who was "very useful to me for ministry" (2 Timothy 4:11).

May I be like Barnabas, who sees people beyond their past failures. May I be like Barnabas, who is generous not only with his possessions, but also with his encouragement and opportunities. May I be like Barnabas, who rejoices in seeing God's grace at work in another person's life instead of becoming jealous. May I be like Barnabas, who shows support not only with words, but also with actions.
