I shared with a friend today how, since early this year, I have felt an increasing sense of urgency stirring in my heart, almost as though time is running out and Jesus could return at any moment. This has made me more conscious of how I steward the time God has given me and deepened my desire to make the most of every opportunity and be as productive as I can for His Kingdom, although I still struggle to live in a way that reflects this desire.

My friend then posed a seemingly simple question, one that I still find myself continually asking God for discernment about: "How do we define what is productive?"

She went on to share how, for her, spending the afternoon talking about God over lunch had felt productive. Yes, we had not gone out evangelizing or done anything of that sort, but I certainly agreed with her. I felt that we had chosen the better portion. And that got me thinking: just how often do I measure productivity by how much I have tangibly accomplished? For example, how many times have I gone out evangelizing this month? How many hours have I spent serving at church or interceding for others in prayer? These are certainly worthwhile things, but I was reminded of the story of Mary and Martha in Luke 10.

When Jesus came to visit their home, Martha was occupied with all the preparations and tasks that needed to be done to serve their guests. Meanwhile, Mary sat at Jesus' feet, listening to His teaching. Martha eventually became frustrated that Mary had left her to serve alone and asked Jesus to tell her to help. But Jesus responded:

> "Martha, Martha, you are anxious and troubled about many things, but one thing is necessary. Mary has chosen the good portion, which will not be taken away from her."

I am writing this as a reminder to myself not to become so occupied with serving and doing things for the Lord that I neglect my communion with Him. Laboring for God's Kingdom can be costly, and I pray that I would be willing to joyfully embrace whatever sacrifices it entails, while never allowing my service to come at the expense of my relationship with Him.

As the lyrics of one of my favorite gospel songs, "Center," beautifully put it:

"Teach us to discern the moment when to serve and when to sit at Your feet."

May God continue to grant me discernment to choose the better portion, always.
