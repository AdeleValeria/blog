I also read a news article about Caleb's parents asking the judge for mercy for their son. His mother described him as someone his peers looked up to, who demonstrated responsibility, hard work, and diligence. To my knowledge, Caleb continues to maintain his not-guilty plea. Only God knows what truly happened. However, I found myself reflecting on a difficult possibility: if he had indeed committed such a crime and continued to deny it, how desperately he would need to humble himself and acknowledge his wrongdoing.

In that case, seeking mercy for him in the form of a reduced sentence from an earthly judge would be insignificant compared to seeking God's mercy for their son. Ultimately, the mercy he would need most, if he were guilty, would be for his heart to be brought to repentance, rather than simply to be spared the consequences of his actions.

An earthly judge can indeed determine a person's sentence in this life, but our ultimate need is to be reconciled to God. Jesus reminds us **not to fear those who can kill the body but cannot kill the soul, but rather to fear Him who can destroy both soul and body in hell** (Matthew 10:28). A life sentence is serious, but eternity is far more serious still.

On the other hand, if Caleb is innocent, as he maintains, I still believe it is wise to seek God's mercy above all. I hold to the conviction that our battles and struggles are to be fought in the secret place before God. He is sovereign, and He can bring the truth to light.

I am encouraged by the support Caleb's parents have shown him. Honestly, rather than focusing on where I stand on this issue, I would rather reflect on what I can learn from this case. One conviction I am taking away is that I still have much to learn about where to draw the line between loving our family and honoring God.

This also reminded me of 1 Samuel 2–3, where we read about Eli's wicked sons, who served as priests. What struck me was that Eli rebuked his sons (1 Samuel 2:23) but did not restrain them from their wickedness. In verse 29, God rebuked Eli for honoring his sons above Him.

> "Why then do you scorn my sacrifices and my offerings that I commanded for my dwelling, and honor your sons above me by fattening yourselves on the choicest parts of every offering of my people Israel?"

This case has led me to reflect more on what it means to build a godly family. It reminds me of the importance of spiritual leadership within a family, especially the responsibility of a father to lead his household in honoring God. Eli's failure to deal decisively with his sons' wickedness brought judgment upon his household and had consequences for the nation of Israel, including the defeat of its army and the capture of the Ark of God.

If God ever grants me the opportunity to become a parent, I pray that He will give me a heart that honors Him above even my own children. May I never allow my affection for my family to cause me to disobey God.

Truly, we need God's wisdom in navigating these situations. May He grant us the courage to honor Him above all else.
