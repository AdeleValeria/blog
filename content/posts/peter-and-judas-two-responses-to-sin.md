Today's sermon at Church of the City was about the fruit of repentance. Pastor Jon Tyson shared that sin can lead either to restoration or destruction, using Peter and Judas as examples.

Both sinned against Jesus: Peter denied Him three times after Jesus had been arrested, while Judas betrayed Him into the hands of those who wanted to kill Him. However, Peter's sin led him to godly sorrow and repentance. He went out and wept bitterly (Luke 22:62). Jesus restored him and charged him to care for His sheep. And later, if you read the Book of Acts, you see Peter, empowered by the Holy Spirit, boldly proclaiming the gospel to large crowds, performing many signs and wonders, including raising someone from the dead. He went on to become a prominent leader in the early church.

Judas, on the other hand, took his own life by hanging. According to Matthew 27, he felt remorse for what he had done and tried to return the money he had received to the chief priests and elders. And so, both Judas and Peter experienced sorrow over their actions, but perhaps they experienced different kinds of grief. Peter's grief led him to godly repentance, while Judas's sorrow led him to self-condemnation and ultimately death.

The sad truth is that Judas could have turned to Jesus and received His mercy. No sin is too great for God to forgive! However, Satan wants us to believe that we are beyond forgiveness, that we are unworthy of God's grace, and that we should condemn ourselves instead. This kind of lie turns our focus inward, toward ourselves, rather than outward, toward God, and ultimately leads to destruction.

2 Corinthians 7:10:
> For godly grief produces a repentance that leads to salvation without regret, whereas worldly grief produces death.

As it is written in Romans 8, may I be continually reminded that there is therefore now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus. Living in this world, we will get our feet dirty. May I never let guilt or shame keep me from running toward Jesus.
