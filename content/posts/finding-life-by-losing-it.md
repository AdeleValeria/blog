My Bible reading for today was Matthew 10, and verse 39 says, “Whoever finds his life will lose it, and whoever loses his life for my sake will find it.”

Interestingly, I also continued reading The Meaning of Marriage by Timothy Keller. In Chapter 2, he discusses the problem of self-centeredness and makes the point that true happiness comes from serving others. He explains that this is rooted in the fact that we are created in the image of God. By nature, we were created to worship God and live for His glory. Therefore, when we place our own happiness above obedience to God, we go against the very nature for which we were created, ultimately making ourselves miserable.

On the other hand, when we choose to serve God and one another rather than making happiness our primary pursuit, we discover a new and deeper kind of happiness. This is particularly true in the context of marriage. Since marriage was established by God, it is meant to reflect His nature.

Keller paraphrases the message of verse 39 this way: God is saying, “If you seek happiness more than you seek me, you will have neither; if you seek to serve me more than serve happiness, you will have both.”

He also presents a thought-provoking question in this section: “If I put the happiness of my spouse ahead of my own needs, then what do I get out of it?” The answer is simply happiness. A happiness that comes from serving others, the joy that comes from giving joy, and the fulfillment that comes from loving another in a costly way.

Still learning, still processing.
