In my previous post, I shared that I had called a former boyfriend whom I dated for two of the four years of my graduate studies before moving to NYC.

As I was spending my final days in Baltimore, I felt led to give him a call. I wasn't exactly sure why, especially since I believed we had ended our relationship on good terms, with plenty of closure. Still, I felt nudged to reach out. Perhaps I felt convicted that he deserved to hear this update directly from me, given how much he had supported me throughout my graduate school journey. He had taught me how to drive, introduced me to weightlifting, and shared so many research insights with me along the way. He was literally part of half of my PhD journey.

I texted him to ask if I could give him a brief call, and he replied shortly after. We had not spoken in about a year and a half. I told him that I had decided not to continue in my graduate program and that I was moving to NYC. He reminded me that moving there had always been my dream. Before ending the call, I told him that I felt I needed to share this update with him directly because he had been such a significant part of my PhD journey. As I said that, I could sense his voice starting to get shaky.

We hung up, but I still felt uneasy. A minute later, I called him back.

I told him that, as I was closing this chapter of my life in Baltimore, I wanted to apologize if I had hurt him in any way in the past. I had apologized many times when we ended our relationship, but for some reason, I felt convicted to bring it up again and apologize once more.

What he said next made me understand why God might have been nudging me to call him in the first place.

As we continued talking, I began to realize that there was still at least one thing he had been carrying, and that this conversation gave him an opportunity to finally express it. Truly, there was nothing for me to forgive at all. But I am grateful that I called and that he had the chance to get it off his chest. I am also grateful I got to assure him that I had absolutely no hard feelings toward him.

I suppose sometimes, after a breakup, certain regrets only begin to surface much later. By then, contact may already be very limited, leaving little opportunity to express what is still weighing on your heart.

This was honestly one of the two most humbling experiences I have had in 2026. I might share the other one in a future post.

I am glad I responded to that prompting in my heart, even when I didn't fully understand why. I went into the call thinking I was just giving him an update on my life, but came away realizing that perhaps God had more in mind for that conversation than I did. I can only hope that God used that conversation, in some small way, to bring him a little peace.
