Today, I was reading Exodus 13 and highlighted verses 17–18:

> When Pharaoh let the people go, God did not lead them by way of the land of the Philistines, although that was near. For God said, "Lest the people change their minds when they see war and return to Egypt." But God led the people around by the way of the wilderness toward the Red Sea. And the people of Israel went up out of the land of Egypt equipped for battle.

As I was meditating on these verses, I caught myself praying that God would not give me the longer route to my destination, as He did with the Israelites. Almost immediately, I found myself dumbfounded by my own prayer; I paused and thought about what I had just said to God.

From my human perspective, a shorter route means less time spent getting there, which means I could spend more time doing things for God. However, does God care more about getting me to a specific destination and doing this and that for Him than He does about conforming me to the image of Christ? What if the route to the "destination" I have been praying for is cut short, but I have not yet developed the character needed to face the battles ahead? With that perspective in mind, what might seem like a longer and more delayed route could actually be God's way of protecting me from what I am not yet ready to face, because He sees what I cannot yet see.

And I asked myself why I was so concerned about saving time so that I could serve God more, when there are so many examples in the Bible of people whose routes were anything but efficient. Joseph spent years in slavery and prison before becoming Pharaoh's second-in-command. Moses spent 40 years in the wilderness of Midian before being called to lead the Israelites out of Egypt. David was anointed king as a young man, yet spent years fleeing from Saul before he actually ascended to the throne. Paul, too, spent time in Arabia and Tarsus before beginning his ministry alongside Barnabas in Antioch. Even Jesus did not begin His public ministry until He was around 30 years old, and His earthly ministry lasted only about three years.

God is sovereign over time. He determines the number of days I have on this earth. And if He can restore the years that the swarming locusts have eaten, as He promises in Joel 2:25, surely I do not need to be so anxious about the seemingly inefficient time I spend in preparation. He can redeem those years, too, and bring forth even greater fruit in His appointed time.

But this is certainly easier said than done. There are a few things I have been praying for and waiting for, and I might very well be in the wilderness right now. May God grow my trust in His timing. May I be willing for God to work **in** me, and not just through me.
