Today, I attended a PERMIAS NYC (Indonesian Students Association in the United States) Thanksgiving gathering. I had actually been planning to skip the event initially, but felt led to go anyway. And what a God-ordained encounter awaited me!

I had the opportunity to meet an older couple who had previously been shepherding a church here in New York but now feel led by God to serve and care for Indonesian students in the city. At the same table, we were also joined by another Indonesian, who happens to be a graduate student at Columbia University. What a coincidence that she attends the same church as I do, albeit at a different branch!

Interestingly, we found ourselves echoing one another in saying that it couldn't have been a coincidence that we all met that day. The other student and I also independently sensed that the couple were people of God. There was something about their presence that, at least for me, made me sense that they walked closely with Him.

We had a wonderful time encouraging one another, and I was particularly encouraged by the couple's hopeful expectations for the younger generation of Indonesians and for how God might use us. I shared their conviction that university campuses are such important mission fields. For many young people, moving away from home for the first time, whether to a different city or an entirely different country, marks a season of asking deeper questions about life and faith. It may also be their first time being surrounded by people of different religious backgrounds or having meaningful opportunities to learn about the person of Jesus Christ.

I imagine that in Indonesia, depending on one's family and social circles, opportunities to learn about Christianity firsthand may be more limited. Here in New York, however, the incredible diversity of the city, coupled with the freedom of expression this country affords, creates so many opportunities for people to ask questions, engage in meaningful discussions, and explore faith.

Of course, I don't mean to suggest that campus ministry is solely about caring for fellow Christians or reaching out only to those who appear interested in Christianity. That is not true at all, and is far from the message of Christianity. We are called to love our neighbors, regardless of their beliefs or backgrounds. Our love should never be conditional on someone's willingness to learn about the Gospel. I personally have many non-Christian friends, some of whom I consider very close. At the end of the day, friendship is not contingent on whether our friends eventually come to share our faith. We are called to love everyone genuinely.

It reminded me once again that the harvest is indeed plentiful, and that God is at work even among the Indonesian diaspora here in New York. May the Lord continue to raise up and send more and more laborers into His harvest field!
