Today, I checked out Times Square Church for the first time. The sermon was on Luke 17, about the story of the ten lepers who were healed, but only one returned to Jesus. It was delivered by a guest pastor named Claude Houde.

And so, where are the nine?

Quoting the pastor: "Maybe since it happened step by step, they began to think it was their steps [that got them there]. It started with Jesus, but then they began to act and think as if they were the ones doing it. They thought it was their talent, accomplishments, hard work, education, strategy, people skills, and faithfulness."

It just made me think about how susceptible I am to this kind of thinking. When a prayer is answered miraculously overnight, it might be easier to recognize God's hand in it. However, when things unfold gradually, through seemingly ordinary circumstances, I am more prone to think that it was my own steps that got me here.

May I become like the one who recognized that it was Jesus who healed him, and who, in gratitude, returned to give Him the glory He deserves.

A song that comes to mind on this very theme is *Thank You Jesus* by Church of the City. There is a line in particular that I love:

"If everyone forgets to, I'll come back and thank you."
