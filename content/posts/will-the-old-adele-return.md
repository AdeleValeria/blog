It might be hard for a lot of people to understand, but the closer I get to Jesus, the less I become of myself.

For some, that might not seem like a good thing, since it may look as though I’m losing myself. From the outside, it might indeed appear that way. I’ve let go of many things over the years, and even more drastically since last year. Or perhaps it’s not that I’ve consciously chosen to let go of them, but rather that my heart has changed, and with it, my desires. There are things I used to desire that no longer hold the same place in my heart.

As a result, others might think my relationship with Jesus has cost me my desires, hobbies, priorities, and other things they might consider essential to my identity or what makes me who I am.

And despite the criticism or misunderstanding I have encountered, I find myself wanting to become even less of myself. I don’t want my life to revolve around me, but around Jesus. I don’t want to be so consumed with my own ambitions. Instead, I want to spend my life laboring for His Kingdom.

Paradoxically, my life has not become sad or boring, as some might expect. Quite the opposite. The less I become of myself, the more I discover the fullness of life. I’ve found ever more joy, even in suffering. I’ve come to realize that in losing my life, I have found a life far richer than anything I could have built for myself.

So, will the old Adele return? I know some of you might want that, but I hope not. My earnest desire is to become increasingly less of Adele and more like Christ. And if you ever see me moving in the opposite direction, I hope you will lovingly point me back to Jesus and encourage me to seek Him again.

As I shared in yesterday’s post on Matthew 10, “whoever finds his life will lose it” (Matthew 10:39). The more my life revolves around me, as it once did, the more I find myself returning to the very things Jesus came to save me from: death.

I say this as someone who has struggled with suicidal thoughts in the past. I also say this as someone who became a Christian eight years ago, but later became a prodigal and returned to a life of sin for a time during graduate school. But thank God, He rescued me, AGAIN!

I know very well what it felt like to return to my old ways, to the old Adele. Depression, suicidal thoughts, and bitterness returned to my life, even more intensely than before. I know how empty that way of living can leave me, and I don't ever want to return to it.

I have found the life I was always meant to live in Jesus. But it is not simply about a way of living. Christianity is not a self-help manifesto, a philosophy, or just another way to make our lives better. I'm not saying that I follow Jesus because doing so gives me a more fulfilling life. There is certainly truth in the fact that following Him brings fullness of life, but that is not ultimately why I choose Jesus. Rather, it is about a Savior who met me in my brokenness, took upon Himself the punishment I deserved for my sins, died on the cross, and rose again so that I may have life and have it abundantly (John 10:10).

He gave His all for me. And for that, He is worthy of all my life and all my devotion, both in this life and for all eternity. Praise God, there is also fullness of joy in surrendering everything to Him. Even the cost is a joy.

Christ is all, and He is all that matters.

**Philippians 3:7–8**

> But whatever gain I had, I counted as loss for the sake of Christ. Indeed, I count everything as loss because of the surpassing worth of knowing Christ Jesus my Lord. For his sake I have suffered the loss of all things and count them as rubbish, in order that I may gain Christ.
