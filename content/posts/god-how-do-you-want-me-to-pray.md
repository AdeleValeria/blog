Today, in my church small group, we discussed the topic of praying for healing and the uneasiness that can sometimes surround it. There surely can be a tension in praying boldly for someone's healing: What if God chooses not to heal them, or heals them but not in the timing we are hoping for? When we expect to see a miracle but do not see it, what does that mean for the person we are praying for?

I believe that such tension arises less because we think we would lose faith in God if He does not answer our prayer, but perhaps because we are more concerned about what the unanswered prayer might do to the person receiving it. What if the lack of healing causes the person to believe that they don't have enough faith? What if it makes them believe that God does not care about them? Or worse, what if it leads this person to hope, only for that hope to be met with disappointment, leaving them feeling even more discouraged than before? In short, I believe that we care about protecting this person from disappointment.

I would say that I have been pondering where to draw the line between praying, "Let Your will be done, O God, whatever that is," versus, "O God, You are a miracle-working God. I know there is nothing impossible for You, and we are waiting for You to do only what You can do." Even last night, as I was walking home from my Bible study, I found myself reasoning with God for some time. I am still very much learning how to reconcile praying boldly as an act of faith with complete surrender. I remember earlier this year, when I was going through an intense phase of my life, I kept praying and praying that God's will would be done, whatever that might look like. But then, a friend, and even someone I consider more spiritually mature, challenged me with this: "Yes, let His will be done, but what do you want? Because even Jesus asked for the cup to be passed, followed by, 'Let Your will be done.'"

Meanwhile, praying or asking boldly feels more like an act of faith, or at least that's what I would argue to God. Like, God, You say ask and You shall receive, seek and You shall find, and knock and it will be opened to you. And it truly requires faith to take that at face value. I like to argue with God: O Lord, aren't You more pleased when we pray boldly, making space and expecting You to do great things because we trust that You are almighty? God, won't it be more honoring and glorifying to Your name when You answer? I have even found myself arguing with God like this: God, when You promised a son to Abraham and Sarah, and they started to doubt, wasn't their decision to take matters into their own hands a reflection of a heart posture that said, "God, do whatever You want. If You want to bless us with a son, cool. If not, whatever"? Wouldn't You have preferred them to continue showing faith in Your promise? Or when Hannah wept before You and pleaded for a son, would You rather she simply say, "I'm okay with whatever You want, O God. Let Your will be done," or would You delight more in her asking, "God, I know that there is nothing impossible for You. Please grant me a son in Your mercy"?

Although I have been increasingly convicted that I ought to pray boldly whenever I can, last night I still found myself asking God for wisdom on when to pray boldly and when to completely surrender, as if I still think there are circumstances when the right thing to do is simply to bury what is in my heart. Or could both exist at the same time? I asked God. Could I be completely surrendered, yet have great and hopeful expectations at the same time?

And then, almost immediately, I found myself expounding on Matthew 6:33 in my prayer. It dawned on me that I need to be completely surrendered to be able to seek first His Kingdom and His righteousness. And at the same time, believing that all these things will be added to me requires such great faith, too. I'm not sure how it is all relevant to the topic of bold prayer or why that verse was the only thing that came to my mind, but I felt that God was telling me that yes, I can surrender completely and at the same time have hopeful expectations. But most importantly, this reminded me to just seek first His Kingdom. Like, it doesn't have to be complicated: just focus on God.

And that changed my perspective on whether or not to pray boldly. I just need to ask myself: What kind of prayer is more aligned with Matthew 6:33? And that is so freeing. I don't need to carry the burden of making sure that the prayer will be answered in a certain way. I just need to see myself as a vessel for the glory of God, because that is my job: to seek first His Kingdom and His righteousness. And if God chooses not to answer in the way I hope, at least I have sought, and can continue seeking.

Why am I more concerned about protecting others from feeling disappointed in God than God Himself? Do I somehow believe that I care more about God's glory than God Himself does? Growing up in Indonesia, we would hear news about these rallies whose names, if directly translated into English, meant "defending [insert a specific religion here; won't say it, haha]." We used to joke, "Why do we even need to defend God (as if God cannot defend Himself)???"

At the end of the day, I trust that God cares more about the person I am praying for than I do, and He wants what is best for that person even more than I do. And so I don't need to overcomplicate my role. I just need to seek, and seek boldly.

But that's easier said than done, because in the end, you would probably still find me praying to God like this: "God, how do You want me to pray? Do You want me to show faith, or would You rather I just say, 'Let Your will be done'?" May God have mercy on me and continue to grant me wisdom.
