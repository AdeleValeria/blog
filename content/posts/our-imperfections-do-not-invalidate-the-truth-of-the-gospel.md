Today, I had a conversation with a friend that got me thinking about some of my own insecurities around sharing the gospel, particularly those stemming from my personal shortcomings and circumstances.

Sometimes, you see others who don't know Jesus but seem to be leading better lives than you are. But guess what? I've grown in my understanding of the purpose of the gospel.

The gospel is not a self-help manifesto. It is not a guide on how to lead a better, easier, or more prosperous life. If that were the case, then we would be the gods here, and the gospel would simply be serving us to help us build a better life.

But that is not the message of the gospel. Our rebellion against God, namely, our sins, has separated us from Him. Sin has also brought about the brokenness we see in the world around us. And there is nothing we can do on our own to escape this brokenness. No amount of money, career success, religious rituals, or even drugs can save us from it.

However, God, out of His love for us, sent Jesus to die on the cross for our sins, and He was raised to life on the third day. When we repent of our sins, believe that Jesus died for our sins and rose again, and make Him the Lord of our lives, our relationship with God can be restored, and we can enjoy communion with Him.

And guess what? My circumstances and weaknesses do not change the truth of the gospel or who God is. I could fail a job interview while someone who is not a Christian succeeds, but the gospel doesn't become any less true because of that.

The gospel is not ultimately about giving us a better life in this world, but about restoring our relationship with God and leading us to eternal life. Even after accepting Jesus, the goal is not simply to have a good life, but to grow in Christlikeness, becoming more and more like Jesus.

**And so, I shared this with my friend:**

I'm still growing in that area (not feeling insecure) too, especially since I've been going out to evangelize more these days. We're all unworthy vessels, aren't we? But our weaknesses don't change who God is or what He deserves. The truth remains true regardless of our circumstances. And if the truth depended on human circumstances, would it even be the truth?

**The gospel doesn't become any less true just because we have shortcomings.** Jesus is still the only way. And I guess that's the beauty of the gospel! Like, I'm so grateful that my identity isn't defined by how things are going in my life, but by the righteousness that comes through faith in Christ.

Anyway, I'm writing this while listening to this on repeat.
