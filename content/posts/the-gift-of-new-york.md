My Monday small group hosted a Thanksgiving Friendsgiving dinner today, and it was such a feast: meat lasagna, roast chicken, Brussels sprouts with bacon, cornbread casserole, sweet kale salad, mashed potatoes, and pumpkin pie.

One of our prompts around the table was: "What's one unexpected gift or moment of joy from this past year that you're especially thankful for?"

My answer was obvious: getting to move to New York City.

I don't know exactly what has drawn me to this city so intensely. Perhaps I've always seen NYC, and the US in general, as the capital of the world. Having grown up on Bangka Island, in Indonesia's relatively rural Bangka Belitung province, getting to call NYC home feels like the epitome of the American Dream.

But I would like to think it's about something more than that. I don't consider myself particularly driven by the American Dream, the prospect of living in a metropolitan city, or the opportunity to make a living in a first-world country. If God wanted to send me somewhere else tomorrow, even to a slum, I would be willing to go, and I would pray that He would give me the grace to obey.

And so, I honestly find myself perplexed by this deep longing I've had to move to NYC, a desire that has been with me ever since my undergraduate years. I don't quite know where it comes from or why this particular city, out of all the cities in the world, has occupied such a special place in my heart. Sometimes, I like to think that perhaps God placed this desire in my heart because He has work for me to do in this city.

I shared with my small group that I am, in many ways, living in an answered prayer. Long before I ever had the opportunity to set foot in this country, I would spend time watching YouTube walkthrough videos of Times Square. I even remember praying that God would not let me die before I got the chance to see New York City with my own eyes.

I was also reminded of an experience during my first week of graduate school at Johns Hopkins in 2022. At a welcoming event hosted by the Graduate Christian Fellowship, one of our icebreaker questions was: "What's one thing you've always wanted to do?" As cringey as it may sound (and perhaps a little lame compared to, say, skydiving), I told the group that I really wanted to visit NYC. Their response was something along the lines of, "You should definitely go! It's only three hours away from Baltimore."

Little did I know that, four years later, I wouldn't just be visiting NYC. I'd be living here. I honestly don't know for how long, but that doesn't really matter. I'm grateful to be here today. And perhaps the most interesting part is that I didn't intentionally plan for things to unfold this way. As I was discerning what to do with my life next over the summer, circumstances somehow lined up, and the very next door that opened was the one leading to NYC. In less than a week, I went from being unsure whether to stay in my PhD program, leave altogether, or take a leave of absence to moving to New York City with a Master's degree in hand.

But you might be surprised to know that I hadn't actually held on to this dream throughout my four years in Baltimore. Somewhere along the way, amidst graduate school and everything else that came with it, I had nearly forgotten about my desire to move to NYC, until someone reminded me of it.

As I was closing the chapter on my PhD, I felt nudged to reach out to a former boyfriend whom I had dated from my first year of the program through my third. He had been a significant part of my graduate school journey, and I felt that he deserved to hear the news that I was starting a new chapter in life.

It was the first time we'd spoken in a year and a half. When I told him I was moving to NYC, he immediately congratulated me and reminded me that moving to NYC had always been my dream. He's right.

Even when I had forgotten my dream, God did not!

Back at the Thanksgiving gathering, someone asked whether there had been a moment during my time in New York when it really hit me that I was actually living in this city. I told them that, about a month ago, I was riding my bicycle across the Brooklyn Bridge when I suddenly found myself in tears.

My heart was just swelling with gratitude to God. He has been so, so good to me. And coincidentally, I happened to be recording myself while biking across the bridge, so this completely unplanned moment was captured on video.

But that wasn't the first time I had been overwhelmed by the realization that I was here. I remembered driving from Baltimore to New York with all my belongings when I moved, listening to The Perfect Love of Jesus. As I approached the city and caught my first glimpse of the skyline, I just started bawling.

I had just come out of a season that felt like a failure to me. Leaving my PhD program made me feel as though I had disappointed God and wasted the opportunity He had given me. In some ways, I even felt that I deserved some kind of punishment to make up for my failure. Yet, in His mercy, God surprised me with the gift of NYC instead.

One thing this season has taught me is that God's grace is more than sufficient, and His goodness is worth trusting. I know this because I've lived it.

Funny enough, 2 Corinthians 12:9–10 have long been my favorite Bible verses. And this season has given me a much deeper, more personal understanding of what they mean.

> But he said to me, “My grace is sufficient for you, for my power is made perfect in weakness.” Therefore I will boast all the more gladly of my weaknesses, so that the power of Christ may rest upon me. For the sake of Christ, then, I am content with weaknesses, insults, hardships, persecutions, and calamities. For when I am weak, then I am strong.

May my life bear witness not to what I have accomplished, where I have been, or what dreams I have fulfilled, but to how gracious and faithful God has been to me. At the end of my life, may I be forgotten, except for His grace.
